Launch Readiness Planner

Turns a brand team's yes or no answers into a launch plan leadership can sign off.

A guided planning tool, not a course. Brand teams answer questions across five pillars and leave with a launch plan they can print and present to leadership.

Open the live courseView on GitHub
Format
Interactive planning tool, 8 screens
Audience
Oncology brand teams preparing a launch
Built with
Storyline 360, custom JavaScript, print to PDF
Sector
BioPharma

Same patternProblem, audience, build, decisions. Every project reads in the same order so they compare like for like.

The problem

Before a new cancer indication launches, a brand team has to work out what the market needs first: more evidence, payer planning, diagnostics, a smoother patient pathway, or education and advocacy. That thinking lived in workshops and slides, and every team approached it differently.

Who it is for

Indication brand teams running a planning session together, plus first time users who need the process explained before they start.

What I built

Teams fill in a short team profile, then answer sub questions under five pillar tabs. Every yes adds a strategic objective and activity to a generated plan, with space for notes on each pillar. The finished plan is laid out for print and saved as a PDF to take into the leadership review.

Design decisions

  • If unsure, choose yes. Keeps a live session moving. Teams trim and prioritise later, in the report.
  • Soft validation. The tool flags unanswered questions or an empty profile, but still lets the team generate the plan, so a half finished session is never lost.
  • Pillars as tabs with status markers. A group can split the work and see at a glance what is still open.
  • Logic in JavaScript. About 260 KB of custom JavaScript reads the answers and assembles the plan, well beyond what standard Storyline triggers can do.

Commercial Data Primer

Makes a tangled data ecosystem navigable by starting from the business questions.

A short overview that starts from the questions a vaccines business needs answered, then shows where in the market the data to answer them is created.

Open the live courseView on GitHub
Format
12 slide module, about 6 minutes
Audience
Commercial and analytics teams in a vaccines business unit
Built with
Storyline 360, isometric illustration
Sector
BioPharma

Same patternProblem, audience, build, decisions. Every project reads in the same order so they compare like for like.

The problem

New team members hit a wall of data source names before they understand the market those sources describe. Distributors, direct and indirect sales, public programmes and third party data all overlap.

Who it is for

Commercial and analytics colleagues who are new to a vaccines business unit.

What I built

Learners explore six question areas: customers, market performance, engagement, market access, field and distribution. They then follow product and sales through an interactive ecosystem map, and select hotspots to see the data captured at each point and where it comes from.

Design decisions

  • Questions before data. Each data source arrives with a purpose, so it sticks.
  • One map, two routes. A toggle reuses the same visual for direct and indirect flow, which keeps the mental model simple.
  • One illustration style. Isometric artwork ties stakeholders, product flow and data points into a single picture.
  • Takeaways mirror objectives. The three closing takeaways answer the three objectives set on the intro screen.

Connected Marketing Course

Moves marketers from one off campaigns to coordinated, data led engagement.

A narrated course that gives home office marketers one shared way to think about data, content and channels, built around four pillars.

Open the live courseView on GitHub
Format
20 slides across 4 pillars, narrated, with transcript
Audience
Home office brand marketers
Built with
Storyline 360
Sector
BioPharma

Same patternProblem, audience, build, decisions. Every project reads in the same order so they compare like for like.

The problem

Healthcare professionals receive around nine industry touchpoints a day, often uncoordinated. Marketers needed a common way of thinking about data, content and channel orchestration, not another tool demo.

Who it is for

Brand marketers in a pharma home office who plan campaigns across many channels.

What I built

Four pillars: insight from data, personalised and optimised content, touchpoint optimisation with the 4Cs and orchestration, and the marketer mindset. Pillars end in knowledge checks, and the course closes with a skills self rating.

Design decisions

  • Test on the model. Learners answer knowledge checks by selecting a phase on the framework diagram, so they rehearse the model while being tested.
  • Side by side concepts. Personalisation and optimisation are taught together as related but separate ideas, to fix a common mix up.
  • From and To. A reveal of what changes for the marketer turns abstract strategy into a personal shift.
  • Finish with reflection. A Strength, Average or Gap self rating turns content into a development conversation.

Supplier Questionnaire Onboarding

Introduces a new questionnaire by anchoring it in the one learners already know.

An onboarding module that explains why a new environmental questionnaire exists, who it is for, and how it differs from the core questionnaire members already know.

Open the live courseView on GitHub
Format
10 slides, about 17 minutes, with video
Audience
Suppliers and buyers on a supply chain platform
Built with
Storyline 360, video, replica platform screens
Sector
ESG and supply chain

Same patternProblem, audience, build, decisions. Every project reads in the same order so they compare like for like.

The problem

Members said environmental data such as emissions, energy, waste, water and deforestation was rising up their agenda as regulation tightened. A new questionnaire was launching, and suppliers needed to understand it before filling it in.

Who it is for

Suppliers early in their environmental journey, and the buyers who ask them for data.

What I built

Research findings, a who is it for section with separate buyer and supplier panels, a direct comparison with the core questionnaire, and the platform's vision. A user guide overlay covers the player controls, attachments and help centre.

Design decisions

  • Replica screens. Learners see the real platform interface in the course, so it feels familiar when they log in.
  • Two audiences, two panels. Buyers and suppliers each open the panel that speaks to them.
  • Anchor in the known. A side by side with the familiar core questionnaire explains the new one in terms learners already use.
  • Guide first. The user guide appears before any content, so nobody gets stuck on navigation.
